## What it is
The first code of the finding assistant described on [[radiology-report-matcher]]: the checked list of X-ray findings the assistant is allowed to suggest, and the program that checks it. A "finding" is one named observation on an image, such as a pneumothorax. The list is the package `assistant/`; the knowledge of one imaging method sits in its own folder, `assistant/modules/xray/`, so that ultrasound, CT and MRI can be added later as folders without changing the core (decision record ADR-0008, an architecture decision record, a short file that records one structural decision; see [[adr-explained]]). It was built from the literature drafts of [[radiology-r1-xray-review]].

## What it does
The X-ray folder has four JSON (JavaScript Object Notation, a plain-text format for structured data) files. Since the correction of 2026-11-14 described below they hold 233 findings: chest 80, fractures and dislocations 51, other bone, joint, spine and skull findings 57, and abdomen and other plain films 45. Before that the list had 228 findings (205 at first on 2026-11-09, 23 added the same day from the gaps the linking pilot found; the sorting of those gaps is in `docs/r1/gap-clusters.md`). Each finding has an English name, German and Portuguese terms each marked verified or not, a definition in plain words, the descriptors a proper description states with their allowed values, the findings it is easily confused with, and its support. Support is one of two kinds: sourced (it cites a source of the file, whose citation, link, licence and reading depth are recorded) or foundational (basic radiology that textbooks teach without a citation, kept only when at least 3 independent open sources state it, by Giovanni's ruling of 2026-11-09). Anything else is refused. Counts on 2026-11-09: 223 findings sourced and 5 foundational, 37 marked weak, 1,041 descriptors of which 155 are foundational; German terms verified 47 of 172, Portuguese 71 of 164. The 23 added findings were read only at the matching sentences of 72 open-access papers (reading depth "part", one "abstract"), and 12 of them are weak. Counts after the correction of 2026-11-14, printed by the checker on the committed files: 229 findings sourced and 4 foundational, 38 marked weak, 1,089 descriptors of which 160 are foundational; German terms verified 46 of 168, Portuguese 70 of 160. Items left out because they were stated by fewer than 3 sources, were not found, or were numbers whose sources trace back to a single origin are listed with reasons in `docs/r1/omitted-*.md`.

Correction after the fact-check of the first real reports (2026-11-14, commit `3575a75`, requirement AN-86; a requirement is a numbered promise in `docs/REQUIREMENTS.md` that a test must check). The generic entry is now the parent of its specific entries, and bone, site and type are descriptors.

- Parent links: 47 findings now have a parent, 5 before.

Two of the new sources could not be fetched in full: one entry rests on the fact-check record and is marked weak, one source was read as an abstract only (stated in the commit message; not re-checked for this page).
- The five generic findings had no training text when they were added.

Plain names for the kinds of finding (2026-11-14, commit `87d9fc5`, requirement AN-87): each of the four files has a table `group_names` that gives every group (the kind of finding, for example "bone-lesion") a plain English name ("Bone lesion"), 23 names in all, so that no internal group name needs to be shown to a user.

**Merging and variants are different things** (Giovanni's rule of 2026-11-15, decision log of `DESIGN.md`, requirement AN-106). The merges above join two entries that were the same finding listed twice. A variant is something else: an entry whose name is another entry's name plus a qualifier, such as "focal" or "diffuse". Giovanni ruled that variant entries of a finding are not merged. They are grouped as variants of the characterisation of the same finding, inside the same imaging method. Each imaging method has its own nomenclature and is evaluated separately; nothing is merged, shared or mapped across methods. The variant links were written for the ultrasound, CT and MRI lists and are described on [[radiology-modality-corpus]]; the X-ray files carry no such links at commit `33d7776`. The rule is tested on those three lists by `tests/test_finding_variants.py` (a variant points to a base finding of its own method; no family crosses two methods), and their closed sets of groups by `tests/test_finding_groups.py`; both are described on the same page.

**Findings-only scope and the classification of every entry, 2026-11-15** (requirement AN-150, commit `5fc5bbe`; `docs/SCOPE.md`; decision log of `DESIGN.md`, a row marked corrective). Giovanni decided that the assistant suggests radiological findings and never a diagnosis. A finding is what is seen on the image, in standard descriptive terms: a cortical discontinuity, a pleural effusion, a ground-glass opacity. A disease, an injury or a cause is a diagnosis. So the lists are to hold findings only: a diagnosis-like name such as fracture, dislocation or hernia leaves the list and its visible signs stand in its place (a lucent line, a cortical step, loss of joint congruity). This replaces the rule of 2026-11-10 that kept generic entries such as "fracture", and it reverses part of the correction of 2026-11-14 above.

`python3 -I analysis/finding_restructure.py classify` wrote `docs/finding-list-restructure.json`. It gives every entry of the four lists one of three classes and a proposed action, and changes no list:

- **finding** (F): the entry names what is seen; it stays.
- **diagnosis-like** (D): the entry names a disease, an injury or a cause. Proposed actions: reword it as its sign, split it into several signs, merge it into an existing sign entry, or move its content to the entries that hold the signs.
- **borderline** (B): a standard radiological term that also names a condition, such as scoliosis. By Giovanni's rule of the same day it stays a finding only when it has a description of the image that defines it; when that definition rests on a measurement on the image (an angle, a distance), the entry must say what to measure and the criterion.

## How it does it
`python3 -I assistant/inventory.py check assistant/modules/xray/*.json` validates the files and prints the counts. The checker needs only the Python standard library. The requirements AN-61 and FA-4 are tested by `tests/test_finding_inventory.py`, including a test over the real X-ray files, and the quality gate (the one script that decides whether the code passes) compiles the package. AN-86, AN-87 and AN-105 are tested in `tests/test_finding_links.py`, against the committed X-ray files.

`analysis/restructure_modalities.py` (requirements AN-182 to AN-184) does for ultrasound, CT and MRI what `finding_restructure.py apply` did for X-ray, each method on its own list:

1. **propose**: a first model (Sonnet) reads a diagnosis-like entry and the findings of its own body area and names the listed findings that are its direct appearance on the image, and at most one new visible sign when the list has none.
2. **check**: a second model (Haiku) says for every pair whether the sign is a direct visible appearance of the entry and is itself named by what is seen.
3. **apply**, by code: a sign counts only when both models agree. The entry leaves the list and resolves through `replaces` on its first sign. A new sign takes the sources, group and descriptors of the entries it stands for and may carry no diagnosis word (tear, fracture, -itis) in its name. An entry with no agreed sign leaves without a successor; since commit `4c7ebed` this holds also for an entry that has a defining description. A group left with no entry leaves the file's group names, and a lay word moved to the successor loses its marking as a swap.
4. **umbrella**: the split of an umbrella entry, read by hand in the cited full texts (so far the X-ray post-operative entry).

Requirement AN-185 belongs with it: in a method's training corpus, a finding both annotators stated counts only while it is on the list, and `analysis/modality_corpus.py annotate --stale` reads again exactly the texts whose annotation names an entry that left. `tests/test_restructure_modalities.py` (7 tests) covers AN-182 to AN-185 with invented lists and simulated models; the full suite (503 tests) passed at commit `4c7ebed`. Since that commit, a corpus rewrite counts only while its pair is still agreed, so no training row keeps the label of an entry that left.

## Functions
- `inventory.validate`: return the problems of one area file: missing fields, entries that are neither sourced nor foundational, unknown source keys, foundational counts below 3, missing English names, malformed German or Portuguese terms, duplicate ids, unknown references.
- `inventory.report`: count findings by support, weak marks, descriptors and verified terms per language.
- `finding_restructure.classify`, `apply(method)`, `borderline(method)`, `remap_lexicons(method)` (in `analysis/finding_restructure.py`): write the class and action of every list entry; apply the replacements to one method's list (X-ray so far); write the defining descriptions of kept borderline entries; make the word lists name listed findings only. Tested by `tests/test_finding_restructure.py` (seven tests).
- `restructure_modalities.propose`, `check`, `agreed`, `apply(method)`, `umbrella(method)` (in `analysis/restructure_modalities.py`): the two model readings, the pairs both hold, the change of one method's list, and the split of umbrella entries.
- `finding_links.aliases` (in `analysis/finding_links.py`): the map from a merged finding's old identifier to the surviving one, read from the `replaces` fields.
- `inventory.load`, `inventory.main`: read several area files, check references across them, and run the check from the command line (exit code 1 when anything is refused).

## Where it sits in the pipeline
After the literature review ([[radiology-r1-xray-review]], step R1) and before the test sets and the engine that will read this list (steps R3 onward, see [[radiology-analysis]]).

## Who receives its output
The lists of ultrasound, CT and MRI are written in this same format by [[radiology-modality-corpus]], from the article finding tags instead of a literature review; since 2026-11-14 they also carry descriptors, parent links and lists of easily confused findings, made by a different method that the same page describes. The later test sets and engine of the finding assistant, and Giovanni, who reviews the omitted-item lists.
