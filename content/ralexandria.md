## What it is
A project, started on 2026-11-19, to build a complete radiology encyclopedia as a website, in English together with a reference bank of the literature behind it. It lives in the folder `~/dev/ralexandria` (a git repository, private on GitHub as `yujideluca/ralexandria`, branch `main` only) and reads the data of the [[radiology-report-matcher]] project (the downloaded Radiopaedia articles and cases, the book texts and the reference vocabularies) without changing it.

Giovanni's decisions of 2026-11-19: the project is English only; it is a website with an Elm front end and an Elixir back end (the same stack as the wiki), using schematic diagrams as in the wiki where they help; there is one entry per concept (a disease, an imaging finding or sign, a technique, a piece of anatomy); every entry also explains the biophysics under the finding, meaning how the patient's biology and the physics of the device together produce the look of the image; all text is written in our own words with citations, so Radiopaedia is one source among many and nothing is copied from it; only whole articles count as sources, never abstracts or summaries; any source may be used, politely (it obeys each site's crawling rules, waits 10 seconds between requests and never works around a block).

Giovanni's scope decisions of 2026-11-20 (file `docs/SCOPE.md`): the readers are people all over the world who want to learn radiology, and Giovanni studies with it during his specialist training; every concept of the index gets an entry, written in order of relevance (first the foundations, meaning how the machines and examinations work, then diseases and findings, then anatomy and reference lists); entries on artifacts, techniques and machines may also have a section on what is done about the effect and where it is used on purpose (decided 2026-11-20); the biophysics appears only where it is needed to understand how the machine shows a finding (for example why a lesion is bright on a T2-weighted MRI image); a separate Claude instance checks every statement of every entry before it counts as reviewed; animated 8-bit scenes show machines, biophysics and complex ideas, and 8-bit schematics drawn from real images replace photographs; the site is read only (no accounts, comments or quizzes); it is served from this laptop until it is finished and must work well on a phone; one artifact page (a private web page on claude.ai) holds the goals and the progress. Version 1 is finished when every concept of a fixed list of 485 main topics has a reviewed entry (decided 2026-11-25, see "Version 1 list" below; before that day the goal was "all main topics" without a number).

## What it does
Gives learners anywhere in the world, and Giovanni in his specialist training, one place that explains every radiology concept in plain English, with the biophysics where it explains how the machine shows a finding, and with the full-text sources behind each statement.

## How it does it
The site runs locally on 127.0.0.1:4200 (`tools/run.sh`): an index of entries and one page per entry. An entry is a Markdown file; besides paragraphs it may hold schematic diagrams in fenced blocks marked `flow` (a flowchart) or `architecture` (two C4 levels), drawn by the modules `Flow`, `FlowLayout` and `FlowView`, which were copied from the [[wiki]] on 2026-11-19 at Giovanni's decision (not shared; changes to the diagram language must be carried over by hand). The first pass of the concept index exists: `tools/concepts.py` reads the Radiopaedia database read-only and writes `data/concepts.jsonl` (not in git): on 2026-11-19, 17,207 concepts (kinds: 8,348 conditions, 3,311 anatomy, 1,169 signs, 782 technology, 752 syndromes, 712 gamuts and others), 4,010 of them matched to a RadLex id, 1,181 without a body-system tag. Terms that exist only in RadLex or MeSH are not added yet.

The first pass of the source bank also exists: `tools/sourcebank.py build` reads the radiology project's data read-only and builds a search index in `data/sourcebank.sqlite` (not in git) over full texts only: 1,160 open-access articles (body sections only, never abstracts or references), 147 open-licence books, 17 books Giovanni owns (private, never copied or sent out) and the Radiopaedia articles. `match` then looks up each concept's names as exact phrases; a source counts only if it names the concept in at least two passages, and Radiopaedia never counts as an independent source. Result on 2026-11-19: 9,043 of 17,207 concepts have at least two independent full-text sources, 8,164 do not (5,393 have none): those gaps are the list for fetching more sources from the web. Matching by name is coarse: a source may name a concept without explaining it, so "covered" is an upper bound; precision was only spot-checked by eye on eight concepts.

Filling gaps: `tools/gapfetch.py --kind technology --limit 12` searches Europe PMC for open-access articles whose title or abstract holds the concept name (the abstract only picks candidates), then fetches the whole article with the radiology project's resolver (its code is imported read-only; the cache is Ralexandria's own `data/fulltext`, failures go to `data/papers-to-fetch.md`). Pilot on 12 technology gaps: 17 full texts, 15 more concepts counted as covered. But some hits are from unrelated fields (an "acceleration index" in cardiology), so the name match cannot judge relevance: that is checked when an entry is written. Requirement SB-6.

Pilot chapter (2026-11-19): four entries in `content/`: anode heel effect, beam hardening, chemical shift artifact (MRI) and aliasing (Doppler ultrasound). Each has a biophysics section split into device side, patient side and how the two make the image, a diagram, and a numbered source list; each source was read as a whole article or chapter. On 2026-11-20 all four were rewritten from the review findings: every statement carries its source numbers, central statements have two independent full texts, and the sections follow the scope order; the privately owned textbooks are not used as sources. The chemical shift entry no longer shows its pixelized figure, because the original image is credited to a company in its source article, so the article's CC BY licence may not cover it. Radiopaedia was not used for them. The site now renders a small Markdown subset (`##` and `###` headings, bullet and numbered lists, bold, italic; module `Markdown.elm`, requirement RX-8): before this the headings showed as raw `##` marks, seen on the real page. A flow diagram was seen drawn in a browser with a test entry; how it looks in light mode was not checked, and in the dark mode of the test browser the box fills looked pale. Also, `tools/inventory.py`: `tools/inventory.py` opens the Radiopaedia database of [[radiology-report-matcher]] read-only and counts its tables and the files of the book and vocabulary folders. As of 2026-11-19 it found 17,361 articles, 70,527 cases, 7 book texts and the vocabulary files. No entry has been written yet; the next steps are the concept index, the source bank and a pilot chapter.

**Look and animated scenes (2026-11-19).** Giovanni found the first design ugly and the flow diagram uninformative. The site now looks like a translucent "desktop rice" (a tiled Linux desktop): glass panes (blurred translucent panels with a thin sand edge) over a dark gradient backdrop in the palette, a status bar on top with a path-like breadcrumb, an entries pane on the left and the reading pane on the right, monospace headings. Dark mode only.

Visual phenomena are shown as animated pixel-art scenes in the style of the instagram account 8bit_sciences (Giovanni supplied a video; its look: dark stage, pixel art, short captions, labelled parts, charts that fill as things happen) combined with the step-by-step build-up of 3Blue1Brown. An entry places one with a fenced block `scene <id>`. A scene is a drawing that depends on time and a few slider values; the page adds play/pause, a time scrubber and the sliders (modules `Scene.elm`, `Scenes.elm`, requirements RX-9 to RX-11). The first scene, `anode-heel` (module `SceneAnode.elm`, RX-10), shows electrons hitting the slanted target, photons born below the surface, the longer path through metal toward the anode and the resulting brightness of nine detector cells, with a slider for the anode angle. The geometry is real (path through metal is depth over the sine of the ray's angle to the target face); the absorption rate is a teaching value, not a measurement, and the scene says "relative". Flow diagrams stay only where a decision process is shown.

Not done yet at that time: scenes for the other entries, a check of the look on a phone, an option to stop animation for people who ask their system for reduced motion, and Giovanni's verdict on the style.

**All visuals animated or schematic (2026-11-19, Giovanni's request).** Every entry now shows its mechanism as a pixel-art scene instead of a flow diagram. New scenes, each with play/pause, a scrubber and sliders, and each driven by a small formula that has a test (RX-15 to RX-17): `beam-hardening` (rays through a water disc; photons of seven energies, low ones absorbed first; a bar per ray shows the attenuation a scanner reads, which falls toward the centre, the cupping; the bars use a zoomed axis from 70 to 100 percent; water attenuation is approximate), `chemical-shift` (a phasor for water and one for fat drifting in and out of step, and a pixel image where the fat is displaced by field times 3.5 ppm over the bandwidth per pixel, giving a bright band on one border and a dark gap on the other; the 3.5 ppm is general knowledge not yet crossed with a second source) and `aliasing` (sampled wave with dots per pulse, the slower wave that fits the same dots, and a scrolling spectrum whose peak wraps at the scale limit). **Writing order (2026-11-20).** `tools/order.py` gives every concept one rank and writes `data/order.jsonl` and the progress files `data/progress/phases.json` and `queue.json` (not in git). Phase 1, foundations (kinds technology, radiography and, since Giovanni's decision of 2026-11-20 that anatomy is a foundation, anatomy), 4,626 concepts, is sorted by the number of Radiopaedia articles that link to the concept, because a foundation is what other knowledge builds on; it starts with chest radiograph, MRI and diffusion-weighted imaging. Phase 2, diseases and findings (conditions, syndromes, pathology, signs, gamuts, which are lists of causes of a finding, and reading approaches), 11,573 concepts, is sorted by the number of Radiopaedia teaching cases that show the concept; it starts with meningioma, foreign body and pneumothorax. Phase 3, reference (classifications, staging, mnemonics and the galleries of normal images), 1,008 concepts. Teaching-case counts favour cases that are interesting to teach, so a rare disease can rank higher than its frequency in practice. An entry names its concept in its front matter (`concept: <id>`); that makes the concept "written". Requirements OR-1 to OR-4.

**Version 1 list (2026-11-25).** With about 17,000 concepts and about four finished entries a day, the whole encyclopedia would take years, so Giovanni asked for a fixed list for Version 1, based on the contents of the general radiology textbooks. `python3 -I tools/version1.py` measures it from the source bank (the local database of full texts) and writes `docs/version1.tsv` (concept id and title, one per line, in writing order). Four general textbooks are asked: two comprehensive textbooks, one book of differential diagnoses and one open undergraduate textbook. Subspecialty books are left out so that no body system is favoured. A foundation or a disease or finding is on the list when at least three of the four books name it by its title in at least 25 passages (stored pieces of text) together, and it also matters in the writing order: a disease or finding needs at least 10 teaching cases, a foundation at least 10 linking articles. The second condition removes plain words every book uses, such as "syndrome". The concept's synonyms are not searched, because many of them name other things. The result is 485 concepts: 251 foundations and 234 diseases and findings; 16 of them already had a reviewed entry on that day. Only concept ids and titles are written, never text of a book. `tools/order.py` marks these concepts (`v1`), writes their progress to `data/progress/version1.json`, and names the planned ones first in the queue of next entries; all other concepts follow in the old order. The list is not edited by hand and is rebuilt only when Giovanni says so. **Atlas (decision of 2026-11-24).** Giovanni asked for all images of these three sources in the site's look, made by a program that uses no language model, so that it costs no tokens. Two commands do it. `python3 -I tools/atlas_fetch.py vhp|gray|braune|all` lists and downloads the originals into `data/atlas/src/`: about 6,170 radiological images of the Visible Human Project from the library's file server, the public-domain scans of the figures of Gray 1918 from Wikimedia Commons (1,223 listed) and the 282 page images of Braune's atlas from the Wellcome Collection. It sends one request at a time with a pause of one second, an exception Giovanni allowed for these three file servers only (every other source keeps ten seconds); it waits longer after an error and skips files already on disk, so a stopped run continues. Wikimedia Commons answered "too many requests" (HTTP 429) on 2026-11-24, at one second and again at five seconds between requests, after three files. The cause was the program's name line (the User-Agent, the text with which a program names itself to a server): Wikimedia asks for a contact in it and limits programs that give none. Giovanni chose the link to the GitHub profile as contact (commit bedde06); with it the download of all 1,224 listed Gray figures ran through at one second between requests with no failure. `python3 -I tools/atlas_build.py all` converts every original into a small PNG picture that holds only the six colours of the site, written to `data/atlas/out/`. A CT section is written twice, once with the grey scale set for soft tissue and once for bone (a setting for the lungs was dropped because the lungs of a dead body show nothing in it). In a drawing a text detector finds the lines of printed text. Until 2026-11-24 each line was erased and the gap filled with the mean of the pixels around it; on hatching (the fine parallel lines that shade a drawing) this gave soft smeared blocks, and Giovanni said that must not happen (seen in Gray's Fig. 256, the back of the patella). Requirement RX-42 now says that erasing never blends neighbouring pixels: a label on plain ground (paper, an even tone) takes that tone; a label on hatching or across an outline stays as printed; a drawing whose original is at most 640 pixels wide keeps all its printed labels, because they stay readable there (1,119 of the 1,506 drawings). All drawings were rebuilt with this rule (commit af5033d). For the hand-made figures of entries, `tools/pixelize.py` removes on hatching only the thin dark strokes of the letters and keeps the tones under them. The detector is RapidOCR, a small neural network that runs on the laptop (not a language model); it lives in its own Python environment `data/ocr-venv`, created once by `tools/install-ocr.sh`, and `tools/atlas_textboxes.py` stores its rectangles per original in `data/atlas/boxes/`. Without that environment the build falls back to the text reader tesseract and says so. Measured against 248 shapes marked by hand on Gray figures, tesseract covers 37 to 45 % and the detector 66 %; the hand shapes also contain the thin lines that lead from a label to its structure, which stay in the picture, so on the two figures looked at no printed word was left although the number is far from 100. On a page of Braune the program paints over the text it could read, takes the largest inked region as the drawing and cuts it out; a page of text, a blank leaf, a stamp or a cover gives no picture, and very small figures inside the text are missed. The build also writes the lists the site reads: `index.json` (the series), one file per series, and `by-entry.json` (the drawings whose title names an entry's title as whole words). Neither originals nor pictures are in git; they are rebuilt by the two commands. The server gives them read-only under `/api/atlas`, `/api/atlas/series/<id>`, `/api/atlas/entry/<id>` and `/atlas/<source>/<series>/<name>.png` (`server/lib/ralexandria/atlas.ex`; names are checked against a whitelist so no path leaves the folder). **Evidence packs and quote pages, made by code (decision of 2026-11-24, commit 4efaad0).** Giovanni asked for a way to get texts for the pages without spending tokens. A program cannot write new sentences in its own words; it can only choose, sort and credit sentences of the sources. So `tools/quotes.py` works by fixed rules, without a language model (ADR-0004, an architecture decision record: the short note that explains a decision).

- `python3 -I tools/quotes.py pack` writes one "evidence pack" per concept (`data/evidence/<concept id>.json`, not in git and never on the website). It holds the sentences of the full texts that name the concept near the start of the sentence, sorted into the sections of an entry (Definition, Anatomy, Radiography, Ultrasound, CT, MRI, Nuclear medicine, Differentials and pitfalls, Other), each with its source, its place in the source and its licence class. Measured on 2026-11-24: 17,207 packs written in about 11 minutes; in the first run 9,334 of them held at least one sentence. It is the cheap first look at the sources before an entry is written; `python3 -I tools/quotes.py show <id>` prints one pack.
- `python3 -I tools/quotes.py pages` makes "quote pages" (`data/compiled/<concept id>.md`) only for foundation concepts (machines, examinations, anatomy) that have no written entry. The sentences are quoted word for word, only from sources under CC BY, CC0 or the public domain (open licences), only when at least two independent sources give sentences, only from a section or source whose title names the concept, and never from case reports. Before a page is written, each sentence is checked to stand word for word in its source. The page says it was compiled by a program and not reviewed.
- Sources under non-commercial, no-derivatives or share-alike licences, books Giovanni owns and Radiopaedia are never quoted; a written entry may use them in its own words.
- Open texts about the foundations (commits 5cd45f3 and 33e8fdf of 2026-11-24). The first run gave only 57 weak pages for 4,613 foundation concepts, because the 898 open-licence sources held were almost all articles about diseases. `tools/openbooks.py` therefore brings four open texts about normal anatomy and about how the machines work into the source bank, again without a language model: (1) OpenStax "Anatomy and Physiology" and (2) OpenStax "College Physics", both in the first edition, taken from the publisher's public repositories at the exact older version ("pinned commit") whose licence file still says CC BY 4.0 (the publisher changed later versions to a non-commercial licence on 2026-03-19, and those are never quoted; the program stops if the licence file it downloads does not say CC BY 4.0); (3) the SEER training modules of the U.S. National Cancer Institute (186 web pages about anatomy and diagnostic tests; U.S. government text, free of copyright, credit asked; fetched with the site's robots.txt obeyed and 10 seconds between requests); (4) Gray's Anatomy of 1918 (public domain). Gray exists only as scanned pages turned into text by a machine, which misreads letters, so the program compares three independent scans and keeps a sentence only when a second scan reads it letter for letter the same and every word is a known word; it never corrects a sentence (measured: 12,072 of 17,278 sentences kept). The science pages of the U.S. imaging institute NIBIB were not fetched, because its robots.txt forbids every robot.
- How to rebuild: `python3 -I tools/openbooks.py fetch`, then `tools/openbooks.py build`, `tools/sourcebank.py build`, `tools/quotes.py pages`. A new open text is added to the list `BOOKS` in `tools/openbooks.py` only after its licence was read at the source.
- Extra rules for these books (requirements QP-6 to QP-8): each book is searched on its own so that old fracture books do not crowd it out; a sentence shared with a copy of unknown licence is credited to the open source; a book sentence that opens with the concept's name counts as being about it; an abbreviation in capitals (ALL, ION) only matches where the text writes it in capitals, so that it does not match the words "All" and "Ion"; a title with a bracketed qualifier such as "Hip (Dunn view)" is not searched as "Hip"; lines without a verb from glossaries are left out.
- Result measured on 2026-11-24 after these changes: 388 quote pages (the program's own count; 0 failed its check that every quoted sentence stands word for word in its source). Most foundation concepts still have no page; the reasons and next candidates are in `PLAN.md`, item M2c.
- On the website (requirements RX-38 to RX-41): the server reads `data/compiled/` at every request and offers a quote page only when no written entry exists for the concept. The entry list has three buttons, Written (the default), Compiled and All, and shows the count. A quote page opens with a red box "Compiled automatically, not reviewed", and the web addresses in its source list are links that open in a new tab. Checked on 2026-11-24 on Giovanni's phone (Chrome over adb: list filter, notice box, source links, no sideways scrolling) and on a 1600 pixel wide screen.

**Animation rules (2026-11-19).** Every scene follows Giovanni's animation guideline (memory entries `design.animation-principles` and `design.animation-craft`). A scene plays one loop and stops at the end; play at the end restarts it. It has step back and forward, a time scrubber, a speed slider (0.25x to 2x) and its parameter sliders; stepping and scrubbing pause it. With the browser setting "reduce motion" a scene starts paused at its still pose (`Def.still`). A scene that is not on screen does not advance (an IntersectionObserver in `index.html` reports the visible scenes through the port `scenesOnScreen`), and one tick counts at most 100 ms so nothing jumps after a hidden tab. Photons and electrons fade in and out (`Px.window`), the chemical-shift movement is eased (`Px.ease`), and exaggerated drawings carry a "not to scale" label. The scene is drawn at 320 px and shown at 320 or 640 px (container query; narrower gutters on phones), so pixels stay whole on screens from 360 px up. Requirements RX-11 and RX-20 to RX-22.
