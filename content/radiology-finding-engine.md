## What it is
The first slice of the program at the heart of the finding assistant described on [[radiology-report-matcher]]: given a free-text description of an X-ray, it returns at most three candidate findings (a "finding" is one named observation on an image, such as a pneumothorax), best first, the first one marked as the most likely. It is built in English only for now: Giovanni decided on 2026-11-14 to perfect English first and to derive the German and Portuguese terms from the English ones if that works, so the German and Portuguese finding tags (step T2 of [[radiology-analysis]]) and the four translator services of [[report-translator]] are paused.

## What it does
State at the close of the X-ray core milestone (2026-11-14). The project plan (`PLAN.md` of the repository) has a milestone "Shared core fixes, X-ray at target". Its closing check was: on the X-ray tuning half, the right finding is among the three proposals for 90 % of proper descriptions and for 90 % of hurried ones after the questions. The "tuning half" is the half of the teaching cases that may train and tune the program; the other half, the "test half", is kept for one final scoring. A "proper" description is the wording two annotators quoted from a case; a "hurried" one is a rewrite of it the way a rushed non-expert would type it. The milestone was set to done on 2026-11-14 (commit `462479e`) with these numbers, recorded in the plan note and not as an entry of the results registry:

- proper descriptions: 92.8 % with the right finding among the three;
- hurried descriptions: 83.2 % before any question and 93.1 % after the questions, 0.77 questions on average.

All three are tuning-half numbers: measured with five folds grouped by diagnosis (the tuning half is cut in five parts so that no diagnosis is in both the training part and the scored part), with a simulated user who answers 15 % of the questions wrongly or vaguely, on the corrected list of findings of commit `3575a75` ([[radiology-finding-inventory]]). They are scored, but they are not a test: the test half and the real reports have not been scored, and the changes below were chosen by looking at tuning-half misses, so the numbers are an upper estimate.

What was changed to get there (each in the shared code, so the other imaging methods get it too):

- **The learned matcher is seeded with the list of findings** (requirement AN-71, commit `29dbf1a`; a requirement is a numbered promise in `docs/REQUIREMENTS.md` that a test must check). A finding with no training text had no learned profile, so the combined model ranked it below every trained finding even when the text stated its very name: 65 of 66 such proper descriptions were missed. Now each finding's name, added synonyms and definition from the list enter its profile, each with half the weight of one training text; only list text is used, never case text. Measured then (tuning half, simulated, five folds): proper 89.1 % to 94.6 % (the commit message; a comment in the code says 94.4 %), hurried before questions 83.4 % to 84.3 %, hurried after questions 90.1 % to 90.8 %.
- **"None of these" counts, and a pick ends the questions** (FA-11, commit `600af44`). One question shows the user the ten leading findings by name. When the user answered that it was none of them, nothing changed and the list was never offered again. Now the ten shown are set aside and the list may be offered once more with the next candidates. When the user picks a finding, the verdict becomes "sufficient" and nothing more is asked. An answer never empties the three suggestions. Measured then: hurried after questions 90.8 % to 94.2 %, questions 0.79 to 0.74 on average. Lists of 7 or 5 names were tried and gave 93.6 % and 92.5 %, so ten stayed.
- **A floor for "fits no known finding"** (FA-12). With the learned matcher, a vague text such as "something odd" got unrelated findings. Now a text that shares no word with the list and whose best similarity to any learned profile is under 0.10 scores nothing, and the verdict is "fits no known finding". Cost on the tuning half: 1 of 1,379 hurried rewrites.
- **Plain words in the questions** (FA-13). Body regions are named in plain words for every imaging method ("bones and joints", "heart and vessels" instead of the internal names "msk", "heart-vessels"), and every question comes with a sentence, for example "What is the margin?".
- **The structure cutter** (`assistant/segment.py`, FA-14 and FA-15, commits `cb0046a`, `5431e4b`, `44a4f7c`). The cutter splits a typed text into structures, one per thing described. It now keeps a weak part of a sentence with the thing it describes ("broken wrist bone, tilted backwards" stays one piece), treats "there is also" and "another" as the start of a new structure, and returns every structure as an untouched part of the typed text with its start and end position, so the user sees their own words back. On the case texts of the X-ray tuning half the share of annotated structures found among the pieces' suggestions rose from 87.2 % to 89.5 %.

What still does not work, stated plainly:

- The cutter over-cuts: 5.7 pieces per case where the annotators saw 2.0.
- The cutter drops lay wording. Whether a piece is kept is judged by the literature words of the list alone, so a piece in everyday words can fall below the minimum and be dropped.
- Most of the gain on hurried descriptions is the user choosing, not the program recognising. Before any question the rate is 83.2 %. In the earlier measurement described below, the two questions about body region and kind of finding raised it to 87.4 %, and the rest came with the question that lets the user pick among ten finding names, and later with its "none of these" follow-up. No committed measurement of the closing state separates the pick from the other questions.
- The simulated user knows the body region and the kind of finding from the target itself. A real user may not.

The paragraphs below describe how the engine was built during 2026-11-14, in the order of the work; their numbers are the measurements of that moment, all on the tuning half.

It reads a description clause by clause, sets apart words that a negation ("no", "without", "absent") covers and words that are hedged ("possibly"), and scores every finding of [[radiology-finding-inventory]] against the stated words. The scoring uses only the inventory (each finding's name, group and descriptor names and values), never text from the cases or articles, so the cases can still judge it fairly. The scorer `analysis/assistant_eval.py` counts a (text, target finding) pair as a hit only when the target is exactly one of the three proposed, resamples a 95 % interval by case, and refuses to score the test half of the data: that half is scored once, in step R8.

The unit of analysis is one structure (Giovanni, 2026-11-14): a text that describes several structures is analysed one structure at a time, each getting its own three suggestions, and several mentions of the same thing count as one structure. For the proper test set a structure is what the annotators quoted for a finding. My first measurement used a whole case text against all its findings with only three slots, which capped the score at 86 % and was the wrong unit; it was replaced.

Measured on the tuning half per structure (1,453 pairs from 718 cases): the right finding is among the three in 74.5 % of pairs (95 % interval 72 to 77) and is first in 57.7 %. The target was 97 % then; Giovanni lowered it to 90 % on 2026-11-14 (`docs/SCOPE.md`). A first run reported 40 % because the scorer reused the first structure's answer for every structure of a case; that bug is fixed and has a test. When the engine misses, the right finding is within its top ten in 80 % of cases: the gap is vocabulary the inventory lacks ("pleural fluid" or "hydrothorax" for pleural effusion), so the next steps are a literature-sourced synonym list per finding and a descriptor reader that re-scores candidates.

Synonyms (`analysis/synonyms.py`, requirement FA-7, no model tokens): the engine can read an added-synonym list, `assistant/lexicon/xray-synonyms-en.json`, built by exact whole-name matching against RadLex 4.3 (the Radiological Society of North America's lexicon, used under its licence: added terms, names unchanged, not endorsed by that society), MeSH 2026 (the US National Library of Medicine's vocabulary) and the fact-checked finding tags of the articles (kept when at least two tag groups agree). The engine uses RadLex, the Consumer Health Vocabulary and the tags, not MeSH, which brought wrong senses. Giovanni accepted the RadLex licence on 2026-11-14 (an "I agree" box on the download page); a copy of the agreement ships in `assistant/lexicon/`. The Consumer Health Vocabulary (a public vocabulary of everyday wording mapped to UMLS concepts, from the University of Utah and partners) was added the same way and brings real lay wording ("fluid in the chest", "broken rib"): with RadLex and the finding tags the top-three rate is 77.0 % and the first-place rate 61.7 % on the tuning half. The alternative names listed in the `keywords` field of the Radiopaedia articles were tested too and gave no gain, so they are not used (their licence, CC BY-NC-SA, would also need a decision). Searches for an open list of informal radiology wording found none: MedlinePlus has only coarse lay names ("broken bone", "collapsed lung"), the Consumer Health Vocabulary needs a registration with a name, and the RadLex normalisation corpus of the Roberts laboratory is not released yet. The effect is small, from 74.5 % to 77 % in the top three on the tuning half, because neither vocabulary has the everyday wording ("pleural fluid", "hydrothorax"); the next lever is the descriptor reader, not more vocabulary.

The segmenter (`assistant/segment.py`, requirement FA-10) cuts a real description into structures using only the engine's scores: sentences are cut again where a list names different findings, a piece that starts with a back reference ("it", "this"), supports no finding or has the same best finding as the piece before joins it, and a piece scoring below a minimum is dropped. At its first measurement it kept 71 % of the true structures and proposed 4.7 per case where the annotators saw 2.0, because word matching cannot tell a finding from a mention of something unlisted. The current figures are in the state paragraph at the top of this section.

Where precision is lost (analysis of 2026-11-14, `docs/r5-bottleneck-analysis.md`): when the text contains the whole finding name the right finding is in the top three 98.6 % of the time, when it contains part of the name 82.5 %, and when the text only describes the finding without naming it only 26.6 % (244 of 1,453 structures, 54 % of all misses). The engine is a bag of words: it does not tell "nodular" from "nodule", does not know which structure a sentence is about or where in the body it is, and does not know what a finding looks like. Knowing the body region would add at most 3 points; more vocabulary, sub-class terms and better word forms add about nothing. Eight findings (opacity, orthopaedic implant, atelectasis, enteric tube, osteoarthritic joint change, soft-tissue calcification, pulmonary mass, endotracheal tube) carry 46 % of the misses. The next development steps are: a descriptor reader and semantic matcher (location, shape, density, device type scored against each finding's schema; a biomedical sentence-embedding model is the heavier option and needs Giovanni's approval), generic-finding and abbreviation logic, a body-region gate, an audit of how many wrong answers are defensible (the label ceiling), a more precise segmenter, and rebuilding the fabricated and insufficiency sets per structure.

Design review (2026-11-14, `docs/r5-design-review.md`): the biggest brake found is the project's own rule that no case text may enter a model. Inside the tuning half, with diagnoses split so none is in both training and scoring, a plain word-centroid classifier trained on about 1,160 labelled structures reaches 83.5 % in the top three (83.8 % combined with the engine) against 77.0 % for the engine alone. The rule was made to keep a comparison of four diagnosis rankers fair; for the finding assistant the held-out test half and the planned real-report test already protect fairness. Label ambiguity, by contrast, costs only about 2 points (parents, children and siblings counted as right add 2.1; 7.5 % of misses have a top-three answer an annotator also gave for the same words). The review also notes that sufficiency and questions, the point of the product, have no engine yet, that the finding hierarchy is thin (88 of 228 findings have no parent, child or confusable link), that the real-report test comes last, and that four imaging methods in three months needs one reusable method. Giovanni approved the change on 2026-11-14: the tuning half of the cases and the articles may train a learned matcher; the test half and the final reports never do (requirement AN-66 as amended; the original rule still holds for the four diagnosis rankers). Radiopaedia asks that its pages are not used to train AI; Giovanni's view is that this program is not an AI, while a trained classifier is machine learning, so trained weights and case text stay out of the repository until the terms are read again.

The learned matcher (`assistant/matcher.py`) is a word-centroid classifier: each labelled structure becomes a weighted vector of words, word pairs and letter groups, the vectors of one finding are added into its centroid, and a text scores each finding by similarity to the centroid. It is combined with the literature-based engine (matcher weight 4), which still scores findings the matcher never saw. It was trained on the tuning half and on hurried-user rewrites of the same structures, written by Sonnet in batches of five (`analysis/fabricate.py structures`, about 1.4 thousand tokens per structure, 2.0 million in all). Measured with five folds grouped by diagnosis inside the tuning half, so no diagnosis is in both training and scoring, the right finding is in the top three: engine alone 77.0 % on the proper quotes and 33.7 % on the rewrites; hybrid 88.1 % on the quotes and 83.2 % on the rewrites (first place 75.8 % and 66.4 %). The rewrites show the real difficulty of loose descriptions, and the matcher closes most of it. The targets were 97 % on proper descriptions and 90 % on rewrites after the questions are answered (the first was lowered to 90 % on 2026-11-14); the test half has not been scored (step R8, once).

The dialog (`assistant/dialog.py`, requirements FA-2 and FA-3, scored by `analysis/dialog_eval.py`, AN-67) is the part that says whether a description is enough and what to ask next. It turns the hybrid scores into probabilities. The verdict is "sufficient" when the best finding has at least 80 % probability, "no fit" when nothing scores, and "insufficient" otherwise. On the 1,379 rewrites of the tuning half, 38 % get "sufficient" and those are right in the top three 95.8 % of the time (the rest 75.6 %), so the verdict can be trusted. When the verdict is "insufficient" it asks one question at a time: the descriptor with the largest expected information gain over the leading candidates, with the standard values to choose from, up to three questions. An answer moves the scores (a finding that lists the value gains, one with the descriptor but another value loses); "don't know" is not asked again. A simulated user answers from the proper description, and 15 % of the answers are made wrong or unknown. The two questions that carry the gain are the body region and the kind of finding (the finding's group), offered with every value: the top-three rate goes from 83.2 % to 87.4 % (88.2 % with the quote as the user's knowledge), with 1.3 questions on average, and to 90.7 % without noise. These two answers come from the target finding itself, on the idea that a radiologist knows what kind of thing is on the film; when the user is made to answer only from text, the descriptor questions lower the rate to 73.8 %, because the quotes state few values and a wrong one misleads, so they need a safeguard. A third question, "which finding", lets the user pick among the ten leading candidates by name (or say none of them): with it the top-three rate is 89.8 % (first place 81.8 %), with 0.84 questions on average and 91.7 % without noise. That is the user choosing, so it is reported apart from the descriptor questions. The 90 % target is within noise; the next ideas are answers that tolerate mistakes and the proper insufficiency set.

Abbreviations and device names (`assistant/lexicon/xray-abbreviations-en.json`, requirement FA-7): the misses of the tuning half were mostly devices and short forms that RadLex and the consumer vocabulary lack (NGT, ORIF, TEVAR, DJ stent, coxarthrosis, "venous diversion"). A hand-written list of standard radiology abbreviations, about 120 terms for 20 findings when written (101 terms for 18 findings after the list correction of commit `3575a75`, counted in the committed file), is read like the other added synonyms; it is foundational knowledge, not counted as sourced. The engine alone goes from 77.0 % to 81.6 % on the proper quotes, the hybrid from 88.1 % to 89.1 %, and the dialog with questions from 89.8 % to 90.1 % on the rewrites. The terms were chosen by reading tuning-half misses, so the gain is an upper estimate until the test half is scored.

The training corpus (`analysis/training_corpus.py`, requirement AN-68, decided by Giovanni on 2026-11-14) is the one formal database the learned matcher trains from. It has 2,832 rows of the tuning half only: 1,453 quotes that two independent annotators both chose for a finding, and 1,379 hurried-user rewrites of them that a second pass kept (718 cases, 482 diagnoses, 190 findings; 186 findings since the list correction of commit `3575a75` merged duplicate entries, with the same 2,832 rows, according to the manifest). Each row records its origin, case, diagnosis and finding. The finding comes from the literature-sourced inventory, so the corpus is the crossing of several sources rather than a copy of one site; the matcher is also calibrated with the synonym lists (RadLex, Consumer Health Vocabulary, article tags, abbreviations). The manifest `docs/training-corpus-manifest.json` is committed and holds counts, origins, licences and checksums of the corpus, the inventory and the lists, but no text; the text stays in `data/`. `load` refuses a file that does not match its checksum or holds any row of another half, and the cross-validation and dialog scorers train from it (their numbers did not change). The quotes are still words of Radiopaedia cases, so the origin stays recorded row by row, and any later decision on those terms can be applied by filtering on it.

Insufficiency without model tokens (`python3 analysis/dialog_eval.py --insufficiency`, tuning half, 1,453 proper quotes): with the full quote 73 % get the verdict "sufficient" and 96 % of those have the right finding in the top three. With the words of the finding's own name deleted, only 37 % stay sufficient (54 % of those hit), "insufficient" rises from 27 % to 63 %, and 12 become "no fit". The verdict moves in the right direction but is still sure and wrong on 17 % of the degraded quotes, because the matcher recognises the rest of the description. The proper insufficiency set (a model deletes one deciding attribute per structure) is still needed for the test half, and the rewrites of the test half are being generated for it (text only, not scored).

## How it does it
Each finding becomes a small weighted profile of words and word pairs; a BM25-style (a standard text-search weighting) matrix product on NumPy scores all findings at once, so the order does not depend on the number of threads, and ties go to the smaller finding id. Free numbers (name weight 12, descriptor weight 1, definition weight 0 and others) live in the `Params` class and were chosen on the tuning half. The method choice is decision record (architecture decision record, ADR) 0009; requirements FA-1, FA-6 and AN-65 are tested by `tests/test_assistant_engine.py`.

## Functions
- `Engine.rank`: up to three strict candidates for a text, the first highlighted.
- `Engine.scores`, `Engine.read`: finding scores and the clause reading (stated, negated, hedged words).
- `segment.segment`, `segment.analyse`: cut a text into structures and rank each one on its own. `segment.segment_spans`: the same structures as (start, end) positions in the typed text.
- `Matcher.seed`, `Engine.seed_texts`: add each finding's name, added synonyms and definition to the learned matcher (AN-71).
- `dialog.kind_of`, `dialog.question_text`: the plain-word body region and kind of a finding, and the sentence of a question (FA-13).
- `synonyms.build` (`python3 -I analysis/synonyms.py build`): rebuild the added-synonym list from RadLex, MeSH and the finding tags.
- `Engine.with_params`: a copy with other free numbers, used to tune on the tuning half.
- `assistant_eval.breakdown` (`--breakdown`): hit rate by how much of the finding name the text contains, and the findings with most misses.
- `dialog.Dialog` (`status`, `question`, `answer`, `ranking`): the sufficiency verdict, the next question and the effect of an answer on the ranking; `dialog.read_value` reads which standard value a text states.
- `training_corpus.build`, `load`, `train`: write the corpus and its manifest, read it back with the checksum and split checks, fit the production matcher from it.
- `dialog_eval.insufficiency`, `dialog_eval.delete_name`: verdicts on full quotes and on quotes without the finding's name words.
- `dialog_eval.evaluate`: run the simulated user over the tuning-half rewrites and report top-3 before and after.
- `assistant_eval.score`, `summarize`, `interval`: strict top-3 and top-1 hits and their intervals; `proper_pairs`, `fabricated_pairs` load the frozen sets and refuse the test half.
- Experiment of 2026-11-14, `analysis/books_corpus.py`: `analysis/books_corpus.py` builds a second training set from seven radiology books that are free to reuse (IDKD Springer series, licence CC BY 4.0), and compares it with the current corpus on the tuning half only. A sentence is labelled with every finding whose name or synonym it contains, which is a weak label. Result, in `docs/books-corpus-comparison.md`: the books alone score far below the current corpus (49.8 % against 89.1 % top-3 on quotes) and adding them to the current corpus does not help (rewrites drop from 83.4 % to 71.3 %). The current corpus stays; the books are better suited to the inventory than to training the matcher. Book text stays in `data/books-corpus` and out of git.

## Where it sits in the pipeline
The training texts for ultrasound, CT and MRI are built by [[radiology-modality-corpus]]. After the inventory and the frozen test sets (step R4) and before the sufficiency check and question chooser (steps R6, R7). Stage 2, a descriptor reader that re-scores the top candidates, is not built yet.
