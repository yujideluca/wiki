## What it is
The first slice of the program at the heart of the finding assistant described on [[radiology-report-matcher]]: given a free-text description of one thing seen on an X-ray, ultrasound, CT (computed tomography) or MRI (magnetic resonance imaging) image, it returns at most three candidate findings (a "finding" is one named observation on an image, such as a pneumothorax), best first, the first one marked as the most likely. It is built in English only for now: Giovanni decided on 2026-11-14 to perfect English first and to derive the German and Portuguese terms from the English ones if that works, so the German and Portuguese finding tags (step T2 of [[radiology-analysis]]) and the four translator services of [[report-translator]] are paused.

## What it does
State on 2026-11-15 (commit `576a987` of the repository `~/dev/radiology-crawler`). Every number in this part was measured on the "tuning half" and with a simulated user; each is scored, and none is a test result. The words used: the teaching cases are split in two halves. The tuning half may train and tune the program. The other half, the "test half", is kept for one final scoring and has not been scored ([[radiology-one-time-test]]). A "proper" description is the wording two annotators quoted from a text; a "hurried" one is a rewrite of it the way a rushed non-expert would type it. "Simulated" means that a program, not a person, answers the engine's questions, and gives a wrong or vague answer 15 % of the time. Each number is the share of descriptions for which the right finding is exactly one of the three proposed, measured with five folds grouped by diagnosis (the tuning half is cut in five parts so that no diagnosis is in both the training part and the scored part). An "imaging method" is X-ray, ultrasound, CT or MRI; each method has its own list of findings and its own training texts and is always measured on its own, never pooled with another.

**One scorer for every imaging method** (requirements AN-93, AN-95, AN-97, commit `4f1029f`; a requirement is a numbered promise in `docs/REQUIREMENTS.md` that a test must check). The two scoring programs, `analysis/assistant_eval.py` and `analysis/dialog_eval.py`, take the option `--method` (`xray`, `ultrasound`, `ct`, `mri`) and then use that method's list of findings, added synonyms and training corpus. The option `--root` scores the lists of another folder, for example the lists as they stood at one commit. X-ray stays the default and the test half stays refused. Every label is read on the current list: a label on a finding that was merged into another counts as the surviving finding, and two labels that become the same structure count once. Next to the official number the scorers print a second one, `top3_family`, which also counts the right finding's parent (the more general finding above it) or a child of it; the strict number stays the official one.

**The question in which the user picks the finding from a list, and Giovanni's rule about it.** When a description is not enough the engine asks questions. Most ask about a property (body region, kind of finding, a descriptor such as the margin). One is different: it shows the names of the ten leading findings and lets the user pick one, or say "none of these". This page calls it the pick question. It raises every number, but it is the user recognising the finding, not the program. Giovanni's rule of 2026-11-14 (decision log of the repository's `DESIGN.md`, row dated 2026-11-15; requirements AN-96 and FA-19): the algorithm is first tuned without the pick question until it reaches a plateau, as close to 90 % as possible, and only then may the pick question be used or tuned. A plateau is defined as two tuning rounds in a row that each gain less than 0.5 points after the questions without the pick (`dialog_eval.plateau`). The reason given in the log: the pick alone lifted hurried X-ray descriptions from 87.8 % to 94.5 %, so it hid what the matcher and the other questions could not do. The switch is `Dialog(pick=False)`. On 2026-11-15 the decision log says the plateau without the pick is not accepted yet, because more training data is coming, and the pick question is untouched and untuned.

**Three numbers for hurried descriptions** (AN-96). Because of that rule, every hurried description is run twice with the same simulated answers, and three numbers are always reported: before any question, after the questions without the pick question, and after all questions.

**Tuning rounds without the pick question** (2026-11-15). Before the rounds, commit `4f1029f` added a fixed bonus (`NAME_BONUS`, 1.5) for a finding whose whole name or added synonym the text states in one clause, so that a stated name is not beaten by findings that only share its words (AN-94). Then three rounds, each one commit (FA-20 and FA-3):

1. Round 1 (`cffaae2`): the learned matcher also scores each finding by its closest single training text (`Matcher.nearest`, weight 3.0). The reason: the matcher averages every wording of a finding, and a rare wording survives only in the one text that has it.
2. Round 2 (`f7f2f6d`): everyday words bring technical words. A hurried rewrite and the proper quote of the same case describe one thing, so a word only the rewrite has stands for the words only the quote has. The matcher learns this table from the training pairs of each method (`lay_table`, `Matcher.expansion`, weight 1.0) and scores the technical words against the list.
3. Round 3 (`6815f61`): the verdict "sufficient" needs a higher probability (below), so the engine stops asking later.

Hurried descriptions after the questions without the pick question, at most three questions, lists of commit `59042cd` (from the commit messages of the three rounds):

| Method | Before the rounds | Round 1 | Round 2 | Round 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| X-ray | 87.8 % | 89.3 % | 89.8 % | 90.6 % |
| Ultrasound | 81.4 % | 83.0 % | 83.9 % | 85.4 % |
| CT | 70.7 % | 75.2 % | 76.9 % | 77.9 % |
| MRI | 71.1 % | 75.0 % | 76.9 % | 77.7 % |

Before any question, after round 2 (round 3 does not change this number): X-ray 85.2 %, ultrasound 74.8 %, CT 65.3 %, MRI 68.4 %.

**The verdict "sufficient" needs a probability of 0.97** (`SURE` in `assistant/dialog.py`; it was 0.80, then 0.90 in round 1, then 0.97 in round 3 after 0.90, 0.95 and 0.97 were tried in the folds). The engine turns its scores into probabilities and calls a description "sufficient" when the best finding reaches this value; otherwise it asks. The new scoring terms of rounds 1 and 2 spread the scores wider, so with the old value the engine stopped asking too early. The reliability check of the verdict was repeated on the final X-ray list and is recorded in decision record ADR 0009 (an ADR, architecture decision record, is a short file that records one structural decision): on 1,453 proper X-ray quotes of the tuning half, 64 % get "sufficient" and 99 % of those have the right finding among the three; with the words of the finding's own name deleted, 24 % stay "sufficient" and 67 % of those hit. "Sure and wrong" fell from 223 of 1,311 such quotes (17 %) to 104 of 1,322 (8 %).

**At most five questions per description** (was three; decided by Giovanni on 2026-11-15, `MAX_QUESTIONS` in `assistant/dialog.py`, requirement WS-9, commit `f4408cc`). The scorers and the website read the same constant ([[radiology-website]]). State of the tuning half with five questions, lists of commit `91cc7ff`, from the plan note of milestone "Ultrasound, CT and MRI at target" (M5) and the commit message:

| X-ray | 94.7 % | 90.5 % | 95.3 % | 2.2 |
| Ultrasound | 90.8 % | 83.8 % | 92.1 % | 2.9 |
| CT | 92.5 % | 78.0 % | 88.5 % | 3.0 |

Going from three to five questions changed the number without the pick by at most half a point (X-ray 90.4 to 90.5, ultrasound 84.0 to 83.8, CT 78.0 to 78.0, MRI 77.8 to 77.3) and raised the number with all questions (94.9 to 95.3, 91.1 to 92.1, 85.1 to 88.5, 87.1 to 89.2). The target is 90 % for proper descriptions and 90 % for hurried ones after the questions.

**Changes in the questions** (FA-16 to FA-18, commit `4f1029f`):

- A finding can belong to several body regions: the region of its own file, the regions of findings merged into it, and regions its entry lists. A finding of the area "general" belongs to every region. An answer to the region question lowers only findings that are in none of the answered regions.
- The "kind of finding" question shows plain names ("Bone lesion") from the lists instead of internal group names.
- That question offers only the kinds that still hold one of the 30 leading findings (`KIND_POOL`), not every kind of the list; on X-ray that is 11 choices on average instead of 23. "None of these" lowers the offered kinds and the question is not repeated.

What still does not work on 2026-11-15, stated plainly:

- Hurried descriptions of ultrasound, CT and MRI are below the 90 % target without the pick question (83.8 %, 78.0 % and 77.3 %), and CT and MRI are below it even with all questions (88.5 % and 89.2 %). The engine work measured that the misses are mostly on findings that have no training text in the scored part; the decision log records this, and the corpus work is adding texts for such findings ([[radiology-modality-corpus]]).
- The pick question is untuned and not yet allowed as the way to the target (Giovanni's rule above).
- The structure cutter (`assistant/segment.py`) has not been changed since the X-ray core milestone; its two weaknesses below remain.
- All of the above are tuning-half numbers with a simulated user. Nothing was scored on the test half or on real reports.

State at the close of the X-ray core milestone (2026-11-14), kept as the record of that day. The project plan (`PLAN.md` of the repository) has a milestone "Shared core fixes, X-ray at target". Its closing check was: on the X-ray tuning half, the right finding is among the three proposals for 90 % of proper descriptions and for 90 % of hurried ones after the questions. The "tuning half" is the half of the teaching cases that may train and tune the program; the other half, the "test half", is kept for one final scoring. A "proper" description is the wording two annotators quoted from a case; a "hurried" one is a rewrite of it the way a rushed non-expert would type it. The milestone was set to done on 2026-11-14 (commit `462479e`) with these numbers, recorded in the plan note and not as an entry of the results registry:

- proper descriptions: 92.8 % with the right finding among the three;
- hurried descriptions: 83.2 % before any question and 93.1 % after the questions, 0.77 questions on average.

All three are tuning-half numbers: measured with five folds grouped by diagnosis (the tuning half is cut in five parts so that no diagnosis is in both the training part and the scored part), with a simulated user who answers 15 % of the questions wrongly or vaguely, on the corrected list of findings of commit `3575a75` ([[radiology-finding-inventory]]). They are scored, but they are not a test: the test half and the real reports have not been scored, and the changes below were chosen by looking at tuning-half misses, so the numbers are an upper estimate.

What was changed to get there (each in the shared code, so the other imaging methods get it too):

- **The learned matcher is seeded with the list of findings** (requirement AN-71, commit `29dbf1a`; a requirement is a numbered promise in `docs/REQUIREMENTS.md` that a test must check). A finding with no training text had no learned profile, so the combined model ranked it below every trained finding even when the text stated its very name: 65 of 66 such proper descriptions were missed. Now each finding's name, added synonyms and definition from the list enter its profile, each with half the weight of one training text; only list text is used, never case text. Measured then (tuning half, simulated, five folds): proper 89.1 % to 94.6 % (the commit message; a comment in the code says 94.4 %), hurried before questions 83.4 % to 84.3 %, hurried after questions 90.1 % to 90.8 %.
- **"None of these" counts, and a pick ends the questions** (FA-11, commit `600af44`). One question shows the user the ten leading findings by name. When the user answered that it was none of them, nothing changed and the list was never offered again. Now the ten shown are set aside and the list may be offered once more with the next candidates. When the user picks a finding, the verdict becomes "sufficient" and nothing more is asked. An answer never empties the three suggestions. Measured then: hurried after questions 90.8 % to 94.2 %, questions 0.79 to 0.74 on average. Lists of 7 or 5 names were tried and gave 93.6 % and 92.5 %, so ten stayed.
- **A floor for "fits no known finding"** (FA-12). With the learned matcher, a vague text such as "something odd" got unrelated findings. Now a text that shares no word with the list and whose best similarity to any learned profile is under 0.10 scores nothing, and the verdict is "fits no known finding". Cost on the tuning half: 1 of 1,379 hurried rewrites.
- **Plain words in the questions** (FA-13). Body regions are named in plain words for every imaging method ("bones and joints", "heart and vessels" instead of the internal names "msk", "heart-vessels"), and every question comes with a sentence, for example "What is the margin?".
- **The structure cutter** (`assistant/segment.py`, FA-14 and FA-15, commits `cb0046a`, `5431e4b`, `44a4f7c`). The cutter splits a typed text into structures, one per thing described. It now keeps a weak part of a sentence with the thing it describes ("broken wrist bone, tilted backwards" stays one piece), treats "there is also" and "another" as the start of a new structure, and returns every structure as an untouched part of the typed text with its start and end position, so the user sees their own words back. On the case texts of the X-ray tuning half the share of annotated structures found among the pieces' suggestions rose from 87.2 % to 89.5 %.

What did not work at that close:

- The cutter over-cuts: 5.7 pieces per case where the annotators saw 2.0.
- The cutter drops lay wording. Whether a piece is kept is judged by the literature words of the list alone, so a piece in everyday words can fall below the minimum and be dropped.
- Most of the gain on hurried descriptions is the user choosing, not the program recognising. Before any question the rate is 83.2 %. In the earlier measurement described below, the two questions about body region and kind of finding raised it to 87.4 %, and the rest came with the question that lets the user pick among ten finding names, and later with its "none of these" follow-up. No measurement of that closing state separated the pick from the other questions; since 2026-11-15 the three numbers above do.
- The simulated user knows the body region and the kind of finding from the target itself. A real user may not.

The paragraphs below describe how the engine was built during 2026-11-14, in the order of the work; their numbers are the measurements of that moment, all on the tuning half.

It reads a description clause by clause, sets apart words that a negation ("no", "without", "absent") covers and words that are hedged ("possibly"), and scores every finding of [[radiology-finding-inventory]] against the stated words. The scoring uses only the inventory (each finding's name, group and descriptor names and values), never text from the cases or articles, so the cases can still judge it fairly. The scorer `analysis/assistant_eval.py` counts a (text, target finding) pair as a hit only when the target is exactly one of the three proposed, resamples a 95 % interval by case, and refuses to score the test half of the data: that half is scored once, in step R8.

The unit of analysis is one structure (Giovanni, 2026-11-14): a text that describes several structures is analysed one structure at a time, each getting its own three suggestions, and several mentions of the same thing count as one structure. For the proper test set a structure is what the annotators quoted for a finding. My first measurement used a whole case text against all its findings with only three slots, which capped the score at 86 % and was the wrong unit; it was replaced.

Measured on the tuning half per structure (1,453 pairs from 718 cases): the right finding is among the three in 74.5 % of pairs (95 % interval 72 to 77) and is first in 57.7 %. The target was 97 % then; Giovanni lowered it to 90 % on 2026-11-14 (`docs/SCOPE.md`). A first run reported 40 % because the scorer reused the first structure's answer for every structure of a case; that bug is fixed and has a test. When the engine misses, the right finding is within its top ten in 80 % of cases: the gap is vocabulary the inventory lacks ("pleural fluid" or "hydrothorax" for pleural effusion), so the next steps are a literature-sourced synonym list per finding and a descriptor reader that re-scores candidates.

Synonyms (`analysis/synonyms.py`, requirement FA-7, no model tokens): the engine can read an added-synonym list, `assistant/lexicon/xray-synonyms-en.json`, built by exact whole-name matching against RadLex 4.3 (the Radiological Society of North America's lexicon, used under its licence: added terms, names unchanged, not endorsed by that society), MeSH 2026 (the US National Library of Medicine's vocabulary) and the fact-checked finding tags of the articles (kept when at least two tag groups agree). The engine uses RadLex, the Consumer Health Vocabulary and the tags, not MeSH, which brought wrong senses. Giovanni accepted the RadLex licence on 2026-11-14 (an "I agree" box on the download page); a copy of the agreement ships in `assistant/lexicon/`. The Consumer Health Vocabulary (a public vocabulary of everyday wording mapped to UMLS concepts, from the University of Utah and partners) was added the same way and brings real lay wording ("fluid in the chest", "broken rib"): with RadLex and the finding tags the top-three rate is 77.0 % and the first-place rate 61.7 % on the tuning half. The alternative names listed in the `keywords` field of the Radiopaedia articles were tested too and gave no gain, so they are not used (their licence, CC BY-NC-SA, would also need a decision). Searches for an open list of informal radiology wording found none: MedlinePlus has only coarse lay names ("broken bone", "collapsed lung"), the Consumer Health Vocabulary needs a registration with a name, and the RadLex normalisation corpus of the Roberts laboratory is not released yet. The effect is small, from 74.5 % to 77 % in the top three on the tuning half, because neither vocabulary has the everyday wording ("pleural fluid", "hydrothorax"); the next lever is the descriptor reader, not more vocabulary.

The segmenter (`assistant/segment.py`, requirement FA-10) cuts a real description into structures using only the engine's scores: sentences are cut again where a list names different findings, a piece that starts with a back reference ("it", "this"), supports no finding or has the same best finding as the piece before joins it, and a piece scoring below a minimum is dropped. At its first measurement it kept 71 % of the true structures and proposed 4.7 per case where the annotators saw 2.0, because word matching cannot tell a finding from a mention of something unlisted. The current figures are in the state paragraph at the top of this section.

Where precision is lost (analysis of 2026-11-14, `docs/r5-bottleneck-analysis.md`): when the text contains the whole finding name the right finding is in the top three 98.6 % of the time, when it contains part of the name 82.5 %, and when the text only describes the finding without naming it only 26.6 % (244 of 1,453 structures, 54 % of all misses). The engine is a bag of words: it does not tell "nodular" from "nodule", does not know which structure a sentence is about or where in the body it is, and does not know what a finding looks like. Knowing the body region would add at most 3 points; more vocabulary, sub-class terms and better word forms add about nothing. Eight findings (opacity, orthopaedic implant, atelectasis, enteric tube, osteoarthritic joint change, soft-tissue calcification, pulmonary mass, endotracheal tube) carry 46 % of the misses. The next development steps are: a descriptor reader and semantic matcher (location, shape, density, device type scored against each finding's schema; a biomedical sentence-embedding model is the heavier option and needs Giovanni's approval), generic-finding and abbreviation logic, a body-region gate, an audit of how many wrong answers are defensible (the label ceiling), a more precise segmenter, and rebuilding the fabricated and insufficiency sets per structure.

Design review (2026-11-14, `docs/r5-design-review.md`): the biggest brake found is the project's own rule that no case text may enter a model. Inside the tuning half, with diagnoses split so none is in both training and scoring, a plain word-centroid classifier trained on about 1,160 labelled structures reaches 83.5 % in the top three (83.8 % combined with the engine) against 77.0 % for the engine alone. The rule was made to keep a comparison of four diagnosis rankers fair; for the finding assistant the held-out test half and the planned real-report test already protect fairness. Label ambiguity, by contrast, costs only about 2 points (parents, children and siblings counted as right add 2.1; 7.5 % of misses have a top-three answer an annotator also gave for the same words). The review also notes that sufficiency and questions, the point of the product, have no engine yet, that the finding hierarchy is thin (88 of 228 findings have no parent, child or confusable link), that the real-report test comes last, and that four imaging methods in three months needs one reusable method. Giovanni approved the change on 2026-11-14: the tuning half of the cases and the articles may train a learned matcher; the test half and the final reports never do (requirement AN-66 as amended; the original rule still holds for the four diagnosis rankers). Radiopaedia asks that its pages are not used to train AI; Giovanni's view is that this program is not an AI, while a trained classifier is machine learning, so trained weights and case text stay out of the repository until the terms are read again.

The learned matcher (`assistant/matcher.py`) is a word-centroid classifier: each labelled structure becomes a weighted vector of words, word pairs and letter groups, the vectors of one finding are added into its centroid, and a text scores each finding by similarity to the centroid. It is combined with the literature-based engine (matcher weight 4), which still scores findings the matcher never saw. It was trained on the tuning half and on hurried-user rewrites of the same structures, written by Sonnet in batches of five (`analysis/fabricate.py structures`, about 1.4 thousand tokens per structure, 2.0 million in all). Measured with five folds grouped by diagnosis inside the tuning half, so no diagnosis is in both training and scoring, the right finding is in the top three: engine alone 77.0 % on the proper quotes and 33.7 % on the rewrites; hybrid 88.1 % on the quotes and 83.2 % on the rewrites (first place 75.8 % and 66.4 %). The rewrites show the real difficulty of loose descriptions, and the matcher closes most of it. The targets were 97 % on proper descriptions and 90 % on rewrites after the questions are answered (the first was lowered to 90 % on 2026-11-14); the test half has not been scored (step R8, once).

The dialog (`assistant/dialog.py`, requirements FA-2 and FA-3, scored by `analysis/dialog_eval.py`, AN-67) is the part that says whether a description is enough and what to ask next. It turns the hybrid scores into probabilities. The verdict was "sufficient" when the best finding had at least 80 % probability (0.97 since 2026-11-15, see the top of this section), "no fit" when nothing scores, and "insufficient" otherwise. On the 1,379 rewrites of the tuning half, 38 % get "sufficient" and those are right in the top three 95.8 % of the time (the rest 75.6 %), so the verdict can be trusted. When the verdict is "insufficient" it asks one question at a time: the descriptor with the largest expected information gain over the leading candidates, with the standard values to choose from, up to three questions (five since 2026-11-15). An answer moves the scores (a finding that lists the value gains, one with the descriptor but another value loses); "don't know" is not asked again. A simulated user answers from the proper description, and 15 % of the answers are made wrong or unknown. The two questions that carry the gain are the body region and the kind of finding (the finding's group), offered with every value: the top-three rate goes from 83.2 % to 87.4 % (88.2 % with the quote as the user's knowledge), with 1.3 questions on average, and to 90.7 % without noise. These two answers come from the target finding itself, on the idea that a radiologist knows what kind of thing is on the film; when the user is made to answer only from text, the descriptor questions lower the rate to 73.8 %, because the quotes state few values and a wrong one misleads, so they need a safeguard. A third question, "which finding", lets the user pick among the ten leading candidates by name (or say none of them): with it the top-three rate is 89.8 % (first place 81.8 %), with 0.84 questions on average and 91.7 % without noise. That is the user choosing, so it is reported apart from the descriptor questions. The 90 % target is within noise; the next ideas are answers that tolerate mistakes and the proper insufficiency set.

Abbreviations and device names (`assistant/lexicon/xray-abbreviations-en.json`, requirement FA-7): the misses of the tuning half were mostly devices and short forms that RadLex and the consumer vocabulary lack (NGT, ORIF, TEVAR, DJ stent, coxarthrosis, "venous diversion"). A hand-written list of standard radiology abbreviations, about 120 terms for 20 findings when written (101 terms for 18 findings after the list correction of commit `3575a75`, counted in the committed file), is read like the other added synonyms; it is foundational knowledge, not counted as sourced. The engine alone goes from 77.0 % to 81.6 % on the proper quotes, the hybrid from 88.1 % to 89.1 %, and the dialog with questions from 89.8 % to 90.1 % on the rewrites. The terms were chosen by reading tuning-half misses, so the gain is an upper estimate until the test half is scored.

The training corpus (`analysis/training_corpus.py`, requirement AN-68, decided by Giovanni on 2026-11-14) is the one formal database the learned matcher trains from. It had 2,832 rows of the tuning half only when it was built (2,982 since a supplement of 2026-11-15 for findings added late, see [[radiology-modality-corpus]]): 1,453 quotes that two independent annotators both chose for a finding, and 1,379 hurried-user rewrites of them that a second pass kept (718 cases, 482 diagnoses, 190 findings; 186 findings since the list correction of commit `3575a75` merged duplicate entries, with the same 2,832 rows, according to the manifest). Each row records its origin, case, diagnosis and finding. The finding comes from the literature-sourced inventory, so the corpus is the crossing of several sources rather than a copy of one site; the matcher is also calibrated with the synonym lists (RadLex, Consumer Health Vocabulary, article tags, abbreviations). The manifest `docs/training-corpus-manifest.json` is committed and holds counts, origins, licences and checksums of the corpus, the inventory and the lists, but no text; the text stays in `data/`. `load` refuses a file that does not match its checksum or holds any row of another half, and the cross-validation and dialog scorers train from it (their numbers did not change). The quotes are still words of Radiopaedia cases, so the origin stays recorded row by row, and any later decision on those terms can be applied by filtering on it.

Insufficiency without model tokens (`python3 analysis/dialog_eval.py --insufficiency`, tuning half, 1,453 proper quotes): with the full quote 73 % get the verdict "sufficient" and 96 % of those have the right finding in the top three. With the words of the finding's own name deleted, only 37 % stay sufficient (54 % of those hit), "insufficient" rises from 27 % to 63 %, and 12 become "no fit". The verdict moves in the right direction but is still sure and wrong on 17 % of the degraded quotes, because the matcher recognises the rest of the description. The proper insufficiency set (a model deletes one deciding attribute per structure) is still needed for the test half, and the rewrites of the test half are being generated for it (text only, not scored).

- **The description in standard form** (`assistant/formal.py`, requirements FA-25 to FA-27). `formalise(finding, text, answers)` takes one finding, the user's text and the answers given to the questions. It returns the finding's standard name; each descriptor (an attribute of a finding, such as its side) that the text or an answer states, as its standard value; the descriptors that are not stated, named as missing; and one sentence built from these by a fixed template. Nothing is invented: a missing descriptor is never filled in, a negated or ambiguous value does not count as stated, and a counting word such as "one" counts only next to the name of its descriptor. The sentence holds only the finding's name, descriptor names, stated standard values and the template's fixed words, so no other word of the user (a diagnosis, for example) can enter it. No language model is used, so the same input always gives the same output. The website shows this as its main result ([[radiology-website]]).
- **Defining description and measurements** (FA-23). Some findings are defined by a measurement taken on the image, for example an angle. For such a finding every proposal and the standard form carry the defining description and, per measurement, what to measure, how, the criterion, the unit and the source (`engine.details`). A measurement the text does not give is named as missing with its criterion and is never filled in. A number the user wrote with the measurement's unit is read as the stated value; it is judged "met" or "not met" only when the criterion has a source and is one comparison with one number. When these commits were made no finding list held these fields; since commit `af3afc6` of 2026-11-16 the X-ray list does (18 entries with a defining description, 11 with measurements, see [[radiology-finding-inventory]]). A separate tool looks such definitions up in open textbooks ([[radiology-textbook-foundation]]).
- **The pick question at most once** (FA-24, `PICK_TIMES` = 1; Giovanni, 2026-11-15). The question that lets the user pick the finding from a list of names could come up to three times in one description; now it comes once. Since 2026-11-16 it also comes last and shows no names (see the next part).
- **A shorter "kind of finding" question** (FA-21). It shows at most 8 kinds (`KIND_CHOICES`), those of the leading findings first, and after "none of these" may come one more time (`KIND_REPEAT`) with the next kinds.
- **A never-propose guard** (FA-28, AN-147). `Engine(excluded=...)` takes a set of entries that are diagnoses and not findings; such an entry scores nothing and is never proposed or offered in a question. The set is not built into the engine: `assistant_eval.diagnosis_entries(method)` reads it from the classification of the lists (class "diagnosis-like" in `docs/finding-list-restructure.json`, see [[radiology-finding-inventory]]). The website's bridge builds the engine without this set, so the guard is not active on the site. For X-ray it is no longer needed since commit `af3afc6`, because that list holds no diagnosis-like entry; the ultrasound and CT lists hold none since commit `4c7ebed` of 2026-11-16, and the MRI list none since commit `ed564e1`.
- **A typed diagnosis word leads to its visible signs** (AN-148). `Engine(diagnosis_words=...)` takes a map from a diagnosis word (such as "fracture") to the findings that are its visible signs. The word is never an output: it is weak evidence for those sign findings. When the text states nothing else, the verdict is "insufficient" and one question asks "Which of these signs is seen?". Since commit `af3afc6` such a map exists for X-ray (`assistant/lexicon/xray-diagnosis-words-en.json`, the words of 41 retired entries), but no committed program hands it to the engine yet; on the real X-ray list a typed diagnosis word reaches the sign findings through the synonym list instead.
- **A normal statement fits no finding** (FA-2, FA-6; commit `9ffee38`). A text that only says nothing abnormal is seen ("no abnormality seen", "normal study") gets the verdict "fits no known finding". A finding whose name says that something is absent ("absent gallbladder") is scored only by a clause that negates. A region that the text names can optionally lower the findings of other regions (FA-16); this is switched off until measured.
- **Optional back-off for findings without training text** (FA-22): such a finding can gain from its closest single text in the list (name, synonym, definition or quoted passage). Off by default.
- **Scorer** (`analysis/dialog_eval.py`, AN-143 to AN-146; `analysis/assistant_eval.py`, AN-141). `--bench TAG` scores proper and hurried texts per method and fold on the tuning half, keeps each fold on disk and resumes after a stop. It also reports how often a generic parent entry stands among the three proposals instead of the labelled finding (strict scoring never counts it as a hit). `--formal` measures the standard form on hurried texts, including the number of sentences that hold a value the text does not state, which must be 0. `--cutter` measures the structure cutter. A second, labelled number counts another variant of the same finding as a hit; the strict number stays the official one.

**Questions redesigned, and X-ray measured on the new list, 2026-11-16** (commits `59eb615`, `9a3b04a`, `61bbacc`, `b4ec127`; requirements FA-13, FA-24, FA-29 to FA-35 and AN-179). Giovanni's rule of that day: the users are radiologists and radiology students who are looking for the term. So a standard term or a finding's name is what the tool gives out, and may not be an answer the user has to choose. What the dialog (`assistant/dialog.py`) now does:

- **One short sentence about what is seen** (FA-29, `QUESTION_WORDS` = 8). Every question is at most eight words long and never holds the term or the finding name the user is looking for. It carries its kind (body region, kind of finding, sign, pick, measurement or descriptor), its answer options and a way out: "Not assessable / not seen" for a descriptor or a measurement, "None of these" where regions, kinds, signs or findings are offered.
- **An answer option is never the term** (FA-32, `HIDE_TERMS`). An option is one of three things: the finding list's own sourced line of what is seen (a field `observable` on the descriptor), a relative form (brighter, equal, darker, for values such as hyperechoic), or a plain word of place, side, number or size. A value that has none of these is not offered, and a question left without options is not asked. At commit `3c3cdb8` no committed list file carries an `observable` field yet, so on the real lists only the relative forms and the plain words are offered; writing those lines is the next task of the session that builds the lists.
- **Five answers at most** (FA-33, FA-34, `OPTIONS` = 5). A question about a descriptor, a region, a kind, a sign or the pick offers the five answers the leading findings make most likely. A descriptor with one value on offer is asked as a yes or no question. A question that an untrained eye can answer (place, shape, brightness, continuity, number, size) counts a little more (`EASY_BONUS`), so it comes first.
- **Measurement questions** (FA-30). When one of the three leading findings is defined by a measurement whose sourced criterion is one comparison with a number, the dialog may ask for it: what to measure, how, and the criterion. The number the user gives is judged against the criterion, moves the scores and enters the description in standard form as an answer.
- **Choice of the next question** (FA-31). Questions are still chosen by how much their answer is expected to tell. One whose answer fills a descriptor that the standard description of the leading finding still lacks counts more (`FILL_BONUS`). A descriptor whose standard value the text already states clearly is not asked.
- **The pick comes last and by description** (FA-24, `PICK_LAST`). The pick from a list is asked once, as the last allowed question or when nothing else is left to ask. It shows each finding by its sourced defining description and gives the name only after the choice; a finding without such a description is left out. "None of these" sets aside only the findings that were shown. A finding or sign the user chooses ranks first whatever the scores.
- **What is not asked** (FA-35). A kind that is a finding's own name is not offered. A description known from an abstract only is not shown. A descriptor that only says whether something is there (present or absent) is asked only in the list's own short wording, never by its bare name. Since 2026-11-17 (Giovanni, AN-199) that wording may be the name itself when the name is ordinary words ("Is there gas in the rectum?"), once two models agreed it is no named sign or eponym ([[radiology-observable-descriptions]]); a broad value word judged plain in its finding's context is offered the same way.
- **Body regions as a radiologist names them** (FA-13): musculoskeletal, cardiovascular, genitourinary, instead of the plain words of 2026-11-14.
- **The diagnosis-word map is read from a file** (commit `59eb615`): the engine reads `assistant/lexicon/<method>-diagnosis-words-en.json` where a method has one. Only X-ray has one.

## How it does it
Each finding becomes a small weighted profile of words and word pairs; a BM25-style (a standard text-search weighting) matrix product on NumPy scores all findings at once, so the order does not depend on the number of threads, and ties go to the smaller finding id. Free numbers (name weight 12, descriptor weight 1, definition weight 0 and others) live in the `Params` class and were chosen on the tuning half. The method choice is decision record (architecture decision record, ADR) 0009; requirements FA-1, FA-6 and AN-65 are tested by `tests/test_assistant_engine.py`.

## Functions
- `Engine.rank`: up to three strict candidates for a text, the first highlighted.
- `Engine.scores`, `Engine.read`: finding scores and the clause reading (stated, negated, hedged words).
- `segment.segment`, `segment.analyse`: cut a text into structures and rank each one on its own. `segment.segment_spans`: the same structures as (start, end) positions in the typed text.
- `Matcher.seed`, `Engine.seed_texts`: add each finding's name, added synonyms and definition to the learned matcher (AN-71).
- `dialog.kind_of`, `dialog.question_text`: the plain-word body region and kind of a finding, and the sentence of a question (FA-13).
- `synonyms.build` (`python3 -I analysis/synonyms.py build`): rebuild the added-synonym list from RadLex, MeSH and the finding tags.
- `Engine.with_params`: a copy with other free numbers, used to tune on the tuning half.
- `assistant_eval.breakdown` (`--breakdown`): hit rate by how much of the finding name the text contains, and the findings with most misses.
- `dialog.Dialog.form`, `dialog.question_kind`, `dialog.judged`: a question as the website receives it (sentence, kind, options with what the user reads and the term behind it, the way out); the kind of a question; whether a measurement can be asked (FA-29, FA-30, FA-32).
- `dialog.Dialog` (`status`, `question`, `answer`, `ranking`): the sufficiency verdict, the next question and the effect of an answer on the ranking; `Dialog(..., pick=False)` switches the pick question off (FA-19); `dialog.read_value` reads which standard value a text states; `dialog.regions_of` gives the body regions a finding belongs to (FA-16).
- `Engine.named`, `Engine.group_names`: the findings whose whole name or added synonym one clause of a text states (AN-94), and the plain names of the kinds of finding (FA-17).
- `Matcher.nearest`, `Matcher.expansion`, `matcher.lay_table`: the similarity to the closest single training text per finding, and the technical words that the everyday words of a text stand for (FA-20).
- `assistant_eval.build_engine`, `corpus_rows`, `current_labels`: the engine and the training rows of one imaging method, and labels read on the current list (AN-93, AN-95).
- `dialog_eval.plateau`: whether the last two tuning rounds each gained under 0.5 points without the pick question (AN-96).
- `training_corpus.build`, `load`, `train`: write the corpus and its manifest, read it back with the checksum and split checks, fit the production matcher from it.
- `dialog_eval.insufficiency`, `dialog_eval.delete_name`: verdicts on full quotes and on quotes without the finding's name words.
- `dialog_eval.evaluate`: run the simulated user over the tuning-half rewrites and report top-3 before and after.
- `assistant_eval.score`, `summarize`, `interval`: strict top-3 and top-1 hits and their intervals; `proper_pairs`, `fabricated_pairs` load the frozen sets and refuse the test half.
- Experiment of 2026-11-14, `analysis/books_corpus.py`: `analysis/books_corpus.py` builds a second training set from seven radiology books that are free to reuse (IDKD Springer series, licence CC BY 4.0), and compares it with the current corpus on the tuning half only. A sentence is labelled with every finding whose name or synonym it contains, which is a weak label. Result, in `docs/books-corpus-comparison.md`: the books alone score far below the current corpus (49.8 % against 89.1 % top-3 on quotes) and adding them to the current corpus does not help (rewrites drop from 83.4 % to 71.3 %). The current corpus stays; the books are better suited to the inventory than to training the matcher. Book text stays in `data/books-corpus` and out of git.
- `formal.formalise(finding, text, answers=None)`: the description in standard form: `name`, `defining_description`, `stated` (descriptor, value, and whether it came from the text or an answer), `measurements`, `missing`, `sentence` (FA-23, FA-25 to FA-27). Helpers `stated_number` (a number written with the measurement's unit) and `meets` (the judgement against a sourced criterion).
- `engine.details(finding)`: the defining description and the measurements of a finding; `Engine(..., excluded=(), diagnosis_words=None)`: the never-propose guard and the map from diagnosis words to sign findings; `Engine.diagnosis(text)`: whether a text states only a diagnosis word.
- `assistant_eval.diagnosis_entries(method)`: the entries the list classification marks as diagnosis-like, for one imaging method.
